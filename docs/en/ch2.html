Chapter 2. Royal instructions outside the code: the Songnok and Husongnok (1485–1543)

Key point. Seven years after the Gyeongguk daejeon, the Daejeon songnok (大典續錄) appeared, and fifty-one years later the Daejeon husongnok (大典後續錄). Counted in the open data, the Songnok has 71 jomok (headed sections) and 407 provisions, the Husongnok 57 jomok and 350 provisions. Published studies count 428 provisions in the Songnok. The gap probably lies in where a provision is cut, but this was not checked.

Dates of the two books

The preface of the Daejeon songnok was written by Gwon Geon (權健) in the last third of the tenth month of 1492 (「弘治五年壬子十月下澣」). It records that when the book was finished "the title Daejeon songnok was bestowed" (「書成, 賜名大典續錄」). The preface of the Daejeon husongnok was written by Seong Se-chang (成世昌) in the first third of the eighth month of 1543 (「嘉靖二十二年癸卯八月上澣」).

The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (entry "Gyeongguk daejeon") says that when the Gabo edition was made in 1474, 72 provisions left out of the code were gathered separately as a sequel. How that set led to the Daejeon songnok of 1492 cannot be told from the entry alone. Later instructions, too, were gathered outside the code without changing the text of the Gyeongguk daejeon (Chapter 3).

The jomok counts agree with the literature. The Songnok's 71 equals the count of Yun Hun-pyo (2009), and the Husongnok's 57 equals the sum of the per-jeon counts in the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (6, 3, 7, 25, 8, 8). Yun found that 24 of the 71 headings in the Songnok (about a third) revise or add to headings of the Gyeongguk daejeon.

The provision counts do not agree. Im Yong-han (2009) and Kim In-ho (2009) count 428 provisions in the Songnok. The open data has 407 provision nodes, and no way of counting the ○ marks in text and notes goes above 410. The gap of 21 seems to come from a different unit of division, but the cutting rules of the two papers were not checked in the original. Im found that about 10% of the 428 revise provisions of the Gyeongguk daejeon; Kim classed 115 (26.8%) as provisions on the royal house.

Few instructions carry a date

Most provisions in the two books carry no date. Strings of an era name and year followed by 受敎 or 承傳 number 9 in the Songnok and 18 in the Husongnok. In the Husongnok they give date and office together, as in 「正德十年三月十二日本曹受敎」 (1515). Im counts about 82 provisions of the Songnok whose underlying instruction can be dated. In the text, 11 provisions carry an era name; adding those with the words 受敎, 承傳 or 傳旨 or with only a month and day gives 14. The 82 probably rest on evidence outside the text, such as Sillok entries, but this was not checked in the original paper.

In the Veritable Records

In the original text of the Sillok, 「大典續錄」 appears in 36 articles under Seongjong, 8 under Yeonsangun and 13 under Jungjong; 「後續錄」 in 42 under Jungjong and 19 under Myeongjong. Mentions gather in the reign of compilation and the next (see Codes in the Sillok).
