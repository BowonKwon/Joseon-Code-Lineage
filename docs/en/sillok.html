Codes in the Sillok

Key point. In the original text of the Veritable Records (principal editions of 27 reigns, 378,684 articles), 507 articles name a law code. The names gather in the reign that compiled the book and the next. The Daejeon hoetong appears only under Gojong, in 47 articles.

How it was counted

  • Source: National Institute of Korean History, open data "Veritable Records of the Joseon Dynasty" (data.go.kr 15053647, KOGL Type 1). The records of Gojong and Sunjong (15053646, Type 4) are counted but not quoted.
  • Unit: the article. An article naming the same code several times counts once.
  • Text: original text only; the Institute's translator's notes (〔譯注 …〕) are left out.
  • Names: full titles only. A short title inside a longer one is not counted (大典 inside 續大典, 受敎輯錄 inside 新補受敎輯錄). The Daejeon husongnok is counted as 後續錄. Bare 大典, 續錄 or 通編 are not counted, since the book they point to changes with context.
  • Editions: one per reign (the principal edition; the draft edition for Gwanghaegun). Revised and supplemented editions are counted separately in the data.

"Sok daejeon" before Yeongjo

In year 6 of the Daily Records of Yeonsangun, 「《元》ㆍ《續大典》」 means the Gyeongguk daejeon and its sequel, not the code of 1746; in year 14 of the Sukjong supplement, 「名曰《續大典》」 is a title then proposed. Mentions of 續大典 before the Yeongjo records are counted apart and left out of the chart.

What the counts do not say

The number of articles is not a measure of how much a code was used. Articles announcing compilation or promulgation are not separated from articles that cite a provision in a case. The Sinbo sugyo jimnok, Jeungbo jeollok tonggo and Jeonyul tongbo are never named in the Sillok, and that does not show they were not used.
