Lineage of the Joseon Codes

From the Gyeongguk daejeon to the Daejeon hoetong: 380 years of provisions added and revised, followed through the intermediate compilations and their source marks

This page. Results, chapter by chapter, of counting 7 intermediate compilations made between the codes of Joseon in the source-text data: 4,287 dated instruction strings, the source marks over the 143,565 characters of the six jeon of the Jeonyul tongbo, and the 507 articles of the Veritable Records that name a code. The text of the four codes themselves comes in 1.0.

The basic code of Joseon began with the Gyeongguk daejeon (經國大典) of 1485 and ran through the Sok daejeon (續大典, 1746) and the Daejeon tongpyeon (大典通編, 1785) to the Daejeon hoetong (大典會通) of 1865. Between the codes, royal instructions (sugyo 受敎) kept piling up outside them, and offices and private compilers gathered them into a series of compilations. This site counts seven of those intermediate compilations in the open data released by the National Institute of Korean History and follows how each of them named the sources of its provisions.

This version (0.9) covers the intermediate compilations only. Splitting the four codes into layers by the 原·續·增·補 marks that the Daejeon hoetong put on every provision comes in 1.0, once the terms for using its text are settled.
