The open data holds none of the Gyeongguk daejeon, Sok daejeon, Daejeon tongpyeon and Daejeon hoetong as a book of its own (the Jeollok tonggo and its expansion reprint the text of the Gyeongguk daejeon). The four codes were counted from the pages of the Korean History Database (in 1.0 the Daejeon hoetong, in 1.1 all four). The text of the Gyeongguk daejeon reprinted in the Jeollok tonggo of the open data serves in 1.1 only as a baseline for comparison (Chapter 6).

Units#

  • Jomok (條目): the headed section under a jeon; level3 of the open XML.
  • Provision: differs by book. level4 nodes that carry text for the Daejeon songnok, Daejeon husongnok, Sugyo jimnok, Sinbo sugyo jimnok and Jeonyul tongbo; level5 nodes that carry text for the Jeollok tonggo. For the Jeungbo jeollok tonggo the main-text ○ items inside each source group (level4), an unstable unit (Reliability 4).
  • Segment: in the Jeonyul tongbo, a run of text covered by one source mark; the basic counting unit.
  • Characters: Chinese characters in text and notes together, without the volume heading lines (「典律通補 卷之N」). For the Jeonyul tongbo the size covers the same span as Chapter 5, leaving out the six tables of contents as well.

Reading the marks of the Jeonyul tongbo#

  1. A mark follows the text it covers. Marks that follow one another with no text between are joined and then split into the thirteen marks of the notes (「補」+「編」 → 補編).
  2. Carry (main): unmarked text takes the next mark in the same jomok.
  3. Strict: text ending a node without a mark stays unmarked (18.0%).
  4. Resume: text left after a mark inside a node takes that node's first mark.
  5. Combined marks: text with two or more marks is counted on its own; equal split and inclusion are given as sensitivities. The equal split divides by the number of distinct sources; a mark printed twice over one segment counts as one source.
  6. Not a target: the Separate Volume, the tables of contents, the notes and postface, the volume heading lines.
  7. 褊 is read as a miscopied 編 (two places, provisional). Two marks that could not be read (續+䌡, 五禮+禮+補) stay irregular.

Reading the marks of the Daejeon hoetong#

  1. Units: an entry of the NIKH table of contents with no children is one provision (2,509, of which 2,489 in the six jeon). A jomok is a level-2 entry (234, tables of contents and front matter left out).
  2. A mark (原, 續, 增, 補) stands before the text it covers. A segment runs from a mark to the next. Marks standing together with no character between them (4 places) are kept together as "two or more".
  3. Carry reading (main): a provision without a mark takes the mark just before it in the same jomok. Text before the first mark of a jomok (volume titles and the like) belongs to no layer and is left out of the character count.
  4. Strict reading: only text that directly follows a mark counts for that layer.
  5. Office tables: in the jomok 京官職, 外官職, 雜職, 土官職 and 符信, a mark annotates a table entry, so these are left out of the main character figure and counted by marks only.
  6. Corrected from the print: 續, missing from the NIKH text at the head of the Military Code's 雜令, was added. The Rites Code's document forms (the 24 after 文武官四品以上告身式, NIKH ids jlawa_403_0400 to 0630) are read as 原 after the note 下幷同 on the first form; a mark inside a form (增 今則…) is a note covering its own sentence.
  7. Left out: subheadings, NIKH note numbers, seal places (《印》).
  8. Layer in which a jomok first appears: the earliest in time of the marks it carries (原 < 續 < 增 < 補). A mark with no text after it counts.

Setting the four codes side by side (1.1)#

The rules were recorded in the repository before the alignment was run (rules document v0.1); while writing the code that reads the text character by character, the places v0.1 left open were filled, also before the alignment (v0.2). What was changed and why is in the change log at the end of the rules document.

  1. Reading: the four books are read by the same code. The unit is the last item of the table of contents. Volume-head lines (「續大典 卷之一」, 「資政殿 刪正」, 「吏典」), volume-end lines (「續大典 卷之六 終」 among them) and colophons, headings and NIKH collation notes are not text. The 下幷同 of the Rites Code's document forms is applied to the Daejeon tongpyeon as well.
  2. Normalization: Han characters only (punctuation, ○ and ◐ removed); 21 pairs of variant characters made one (並 and 幷 to 竝, 教 to 敎, and so on). Other differences of characters fall under 'characters differ'.
  3. Alignment: runs of 4 or more characters in common between jomok of the same jeon and title (first pass); remaining text of 8 or more characters sought in the whole other book, longest first (second pass, text that moved). The share of source characters found is the coverage. In the second pass, among candidates of the same length the earliest place in the source is taken, then the earliest in the target, and for each 8-character piece only the first 4,000 places in the target are tried.
  4. Provisions and categories: the last TOC items are cut again at ○ and at marks, and pieces under 6 characters join the piece before. A provision is placed by its edit distance d to the matching stretch and its coverage c: same (d = 0), characters differ (d at most 2 or 5% of its length, and c ≥ 0.9), revised (c ≥ 0.5), no match (c < 0.5).
  5. Three readings: carry (main, as in 1.0), strict, and nested (a mark inside a small-print note covers only to the end of that note). A small-print note is a small element inside the text; where the page frame wraps a whole item in small, that is not a note. In this sense 2,561 of the 3,390 marks of the Daejeon hoetong stand inside notes. Whether to make the nested reading the main one was settled by a rule fixed in advance: if the share of Gyeongguk daejeon text under 增 fell by 5 points or more and the share under 原 did not fall. The second condition failed, so the carry reading stays the main one.
  6. Sensitivity: values with the thresholds raised from 4 and 8 to 6 and 12 characters, without the provisions whose collation notes cite the Daejeon tongpyeon or Daejeon hoetong (notes whose text contains 通編 or 會通; a note goes to the provision holding the character just before it, and a note before the first character of a jomok goes to none; each note is read with its own text at its own place, since the NIKH pages sometimes give two different notes the same number; the 156 provisions left out are listed on the Data page), and against the Jeollok tonggo reprint are in the data.
  7. Other counts: 今廢 and 今革 are counted as strings (occurrences, not provisions). Words for punishments are counted in the Penal Code with fixed regular expressions (笞N, 杖N, 徒N年, 流N里, 絞, 斬, 充軍, 定配, 全家徙邊, 梟示; 斬衰 and 絞首 left out). Words naming a code (原典, 續典, 大典, 大明律, 明律, 依律, 以…律論) are counted with 大典 inside book titles left out.

Dates of royal instructions#

  • Strings of an era name with a year or a sexagenary year, followed within 40 characters by 受敎 or 承傳, are extracted; a second era name, a full stop (。) or a ○ in between breaks the string.
  • The Institute's corrections 〔 〕 (replacing the same number of characters just before) and supplied characters 〈 〉 are applied, and dates are sought in the corrected text (so corrections of the era name or of 承 itself are caught).
  • When a year cannot be fixed, it is set in turn by ① the date of the same provision in another compilation (the last twelve Chinese characters before the date, punctuation ignored, are identical), ② the year in the Institute's node title (when the node holds a single date), ③ dropping years later than the checked year of compilation. Step ① uses only years fixed by the string itself or by a correction; years set by ② or ③ are not passed on again. For a sexagenary year that falls twice in one era, the title year (②) is looked at first. The 31 still open are left out.
  • Dates later than the compilation (4) are taken as copying errors and left out.
  • The unit is the string, not the number of instructions. Months and days are not converted to the solar calendar.

Reign boundaries#

The Western year written in the heading of each reign's first-year (元年) section of the Sillok is taken as that king's first year (Sejo 1455, Seongjong 1470 …). A year belongs to the king whose first year is the latest one not after it.

What was not done#

  • No composite index of how complex Joseon law became.
  • Numbers in different units (jomok, provisions, strings, articles) are not joined in one series.
  • No causal wording. Where the data cannot answer — why there are so few dates from the years of Gwanghaegun — only the numbers are given.

Versions#

  • 0.9 (2026-11) — seven intermediate compilations; Chapters 2, 3 and 5; Codes in the Sillok; Reliability.
  • 0.9.1 (2026-11-16) — Chart explanations filled in. Book names in the charts carry their Hangul reading (Korean pages) or romanization; source marks and jeon carry their meaning. Axes are named, with units, and each chart has a sentence or two on how to read it and what it shows. The lines of the dated-instruction chart stop in the decade in which their book was compiled (for the Sinbo sugyo jimnok, whose year is unconfirmed, the decade of its latest dated instruction), and the Sillok chart marks the reign in which each code was compiled. The two books that reprint the Gyeongguk daejeon are hatched. The home, method and data pages have a short head summary, and Chapters 2, 3 and 5 a note on terms. The year of the Sinbo sugyo jimnok could not be confirmed and is now empty in the data file (compilations.csv; it was 1743); Chapter 3 gives the estimated year and two related articles of the Veritable Records. The key point of the Sillok page adds that the Gyeongguk daejeon is named as often under Sejo, before its compilation, as under Seongjong. No other value changed.
  • 1.1.1 (2026-11) — Straight from the contents to a chapter or section: a chapter list at the head of each chapter, a section list on pages with three or more sections, and a link on every section heading. The home page has full contents by chapter and section. On wide screens the top menu stays on screen. Text and numbers are unchanged.
  • 1.1.0 (2026-11) — With the text of the four codes, Chapters 1 (1397–1485) and 4 (Sok daejeon) are written and Chapter 6 gains 'Did the text follow the marks?'.
