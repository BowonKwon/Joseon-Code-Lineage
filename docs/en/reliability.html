Reliability

Key point. At the 183 places where two independent readings of 45 pages agree with each other, all 183 agree with the source marks in the open data. Dates computed separately agree with 149 of 150 strings; the one miss was a flaw in this site's extraction rule and has been fixed. Provision boundaries reach F1 of 0.94 or more in five compilations and 0.77 in the Jeungbo jeollok tonggo. In 1.1, the categories assigned by setting the four codes side by side agree with two blind judges at kappa 0.93 and 0.91.

Rules and samples were fixed before each check. Results that fall short of those rules are reported as they are. Reading and date computation were done by large language model (LLM) coders, who were given only the page images or the source strings, never this site's automatic results.

2. Reading the marks — 45 pages, two coders separately#

Forty-five pages of the six jeon were drawn in proportion to the pages of each jeon (about 300 segments). Each coder wrote down the marks in reading order together with the characters just before each mark.

Method Coder 1 Coder 2
Pre-registered matching (by the preceding characters): agreement 89.8% (95% CI 84.0–94.3) 98.9% (97.4–100)
Same method: marks found only on the page 36.7% 12.4% (7.8–16.8)
Post hoc matching (aligning the order of marks): exact agreement 83.9% (76.4–89.6) 95.0% (91.6–97.8)
Same method, counting one readable character 94.3% 99.3%
Same method: coder only / data only 2.4% / 7.8% 1.0% / 1.0%

Ranges in brackets are 95% intervals from a bootstrap that resamples pages (2,000 draws). The rule fixed beforehand (agreement of 95% or more, marks found only on the page 2% or less) was not met. Unpaired readings gather on pages of official lists and place names, where a mark is preceded by one character or none; matching by preceding characters fails there. The post hoc alignment by the order of marks is exploratory.

At the 192 places read by both coders where neither marked a reading with "?", they agree with each other 95.3% of the time (Cohen's κ 0.945). At the 183 places where both coders read the same, all 183 agree with the data (no candidate data errors; 95% upper bound 2.1%). Every reading that disagrees with the data was made by one coder alone. The marks in the data are therefore used without correction.

Limits: coder 2 enlarged the images on screen, coder 1 viewed them at the default size; coder 1 marked 191 rows as partly legible, coder 2 56. Both coders are models of the same family and may share mistakes.

3. Dates — 200 computed separately#

The coder was given only the compilation's name and the date string and asked for the year.

Group Result
Ordinary, 150 149 agree
With the Institute's corrections 〔〕·〈〉, 10 10 agree
Left open by this site, 14 the coder could not fix any of the 14 either
Fixed here from other compilations or the Institute's titles, 26 the coder did not have that information; for all 26 this site's year is among the coder's candidates

The miss was 「康熙丁酉改以杖一百流三千里 嘉靖三年八月初一日本曹受敎」: the amendment date in front (1717) swallowed the instruction date behind it (1524). Strings are no longer joined across a second era name. One rule was added from the coder's notes: when a sexagenary year falls in two years, a year later than the checked year of compilation is dropped.

The years the Institute's editors wrote in the node titles of the Sugyo jimnok and Sinbo sugyo jimnok agree with this site's years at all 1,723 nodes that have a title year and a single date whose year was set from the string alone. The editors read the same strings, though, so errors in a string itself are not caught by this comparison.

6. Daejeon hoetong — the 1865 print and an independent recount#

The NIKH text was compared with pages of the 1865 edition (Internet Archive, copy of the C. V. Starr East Asian Library, UC Berkeley), read by one person.

Checked Result
15 provisions that take their layer by the carry reading, drawn at random (seed 20261116), looked up on the page 11 found; none of the 11 has a mark on the page. 4 were not located
First mark of the jomok that do not begin in 原 (璽寶, 驛路, 署經, 殺獄) Same in all 4
First provision of the Military Code's 雜令 The page has 續; the NIKH text does not. Added
Document forms of the Rites Code The first form carries 原 and 下幷同. Of the 24 forms after it, 21 carry no mark and 3 carry only a 增 note. The later forms are read as 原
Marks inside office tables A mark does stand before what it covers, but the page does not settle whether it covers one entry or the entries after it. Tables are left out of the main character figure

The print divides provisions with ○ and a half-filled ◐; the NIKH text writes both as ○. The meaning of ◐ was not established (for the 1.1 inquiry, see section 7).

An independent reviewer (a language model from another company), given only the counting rules, recounted from the same source. Mark counts (jeon × mark), characters per layer (three readings × jeon × layer) and the layer in which each jomok first appears came out the same. The one difference was the total characters per jeon: the reviewer counted the volume-title characters of each jeon (about 14 per jeon) as unmarked; this site leaves them out, as the rules say. Shares move by 0.1 percentage point or less as a result. A second reviewer, in the check before publication, recounted the same values, except for 原 and 增 in the Rites Code (0.1 point), because that reviewer also read 用文字式, which is not a document form, as one.

7. Setting the four codes side by side: blind judgments and ◐ (1.1)#

Samples were drawn as fixed in the rules document (random seed 20261117), and two judges (language models) who did not know of each other were given only the passages and the definitions of the categories, not the automatic categories or the rules document. The criterion is a kappa of 0.8 or more between the automatic result and the judges. Kappa expresses how far two judgments agree beyond what chance would give: 1 is full agreement, 0 the level of chance, and a value below 0 less agreement than chance.

Sample Size Judges with each other Automatic with judge 1 and 2 Verdict
Categories of Gyeongguk daejeon provisions against the Daejeon hoetong (30 per category, jeon in proportion to provisions) 120 kappa 0.98 kappa 0.93 and 0.91 (0.93 with the 118 on which the judges agree) passes
Stretches carrying a later mark but found as Gyeongguk daejeon text: really the same passage? 40 all 40 the same all 40 the same all 40 sampled stretches are the same passage (this is not a measured false-match rate of the whole alignment)
Does 今廢 or 今革 abolish an office or post, or a rule? (automatic rule: jomok title and an office ending within the 10 characters before) 40 kappa 0.87 kappa 0.35 and 0.25 fails: no split is given, only totals

A baseline from the same book. Set against the text of the Gyeongguk daejeon reprinted in the Jeollok tonggo of 1706 by the same rules, the NIKH Gyeongguk daejeon reaches a coverage of 84.7%, and 61.6% of its provisions come out with the same text. When the order of table cells and the place of notes differ, even the same text is not found. The shares in Chapter 6 are read against this baseline.

Stricter thresholds. Raising the lower limits from 4 and 8 characters to 6 and 12, the coverage of the Gyeongguk daejeon against the Daejeon hoetong moves from 80.4% to 77.0%, provisions with the same text from 682 to 680, and 'no match' from 87 to 108.

◐. On 20 pages drawn at random from the five volumes of the printed Daejeon hoetong (random seed 20261117), ◐ occurs on one page (three times), while ○ occurs on almost every page apart from the first two. All seven ◐ seen so far stand at the head of a provision in large characters. Four of them stand where, right after a small-print 增 note, the text returns to a provision of an earlier layer, which suggested that ◐ marks such places. Four such places predicted from the NIKH text were looked up on the page: one has ◐ and three have ○, so the hypothesis was dropped. The meaning of ◐ is not yet established. The pages were read by the writing session alone.
