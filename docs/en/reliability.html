Reliability

Key point. At the 183 places where two independent readings of 45 pages agree with each other, all 183 agree with the source marks in the open data. Dates computed separately agree with 149 of 150 strings; the one miss was a flaw in this site's extraction rule and has been fixed. Provision boundaries reach F1 of 0.94 or more in five compilations and 0.77 in the Jeungbo jeollok tonggo.

Rules and samples were fixed before each check. Results that fall short of those rules are reported as they are. Reading and date computation were done by large language model (LLM) coders, who were given only the page images or the source strings, never this site's automatic results.

1. Does a later mark cover unmarked text? — 30 places on the page images

Thirty places were viewed on the Kyujanggak page images (not downloaded). A page number N in the data links corresponds to image leaf (N−1)÷2, front (a) if N is odd, back (b) if even.

In the 16 unmarked places, marks present on the page but missing from the data: 0 (Wilson 95% upper bound 19%). Under the rule fixed beforehand (fewer than two), the carry reading is used as the main reading.

2. Reading the marks — 45 pages, two coders separately

Forty-five pages of the six jeon were drawn in proportion to the pages of each jeon (about 300 segments). Each coder wrote down the marks in reading order together with the characters just before each mark.

Method Coder 1 Coder 2
Pre-registered matching (by the preceding characters): agreement 89.8% (95% CI 84.0–94.3) 98.9% (97.4–100)
Same method: marks found only on the page 36.7% 12.4% (7.8–16.8)
Post hoc matching (aligning the order of marks): exact agreement 83.9% (76.4–89.6) 95.0% (91.6–97.8)
Same method, counting one readable character 94.3% 99.3%
Same method: coder only / data only 2.4% / 7.8% 1.0% / 1.0%

Ranges in brackets are 95% intervals from a bootstrap that resamples pages (2,000 draws). The rule fixed beforehand (agreement of 95% or more, marks found only on the page 2% or less) was not met. Unpaired readings gather on pages of official lists and place names, where a mark is preceded by one character or none; matching by preceding characters fails there. The post hoc alignment by the order of marks is exploratory.

At the 192 places read by both coders where neither marked a reading with "?", they agree with each other 95.3% of the time (Cohen's κ 0.945). At the 183 places where both coders read the same, all 183 agree with the data (no candidate data errors; 95% upper bound 2.1%). Every reading that disagrees with the data was made by one coder alone. The marks in the data are therefore used without correction.

Limits: coder 2 enlarged the images on screen, coder 1 viewed them at the default size; coder 1 marked 191 rows as partly legible, coder 2 56. Both coders are models of the same family and may share mistakes.

3. Dates — 200 computed separately

The coder was given only the compilation's name and the date string and asked for the year.

Group Result
Ordinary, 150 149 agree
With the Institute's corrections 〔〕·〈〉, 10 10 agree
Left open by this site, 14 the coder could not fix any of the 14 either
Fixed here from other compilations or the Institute's titles, 26 the coder did not have that information; for all 26 this site's year is among the coder's candidates

The miss was 「康熙丁酉改以杖一百流三千里 嘉靖三年八月初一日本曹受敎」: the amendment date in front (1717) swallowed the instruction date behind it (1524). Strings are no longer joined across a second era name. One rule was added from the coder's notes: when a sexagenary year falls in two years, a year later than the checked year of compilation is dropped.

The years the Institute's editors wrote in the node titles of the Sugyo jimnok and Sinbo sugyo jimnok agree with this site's years at all 1,723 nodes that have a title year and a single date whose year was set from the string alone. The editors read the same strings, though, so errors in a string itself are not caught by this comparison.

A coder divided into provisions the text of each jomok, with node boundaries, numbers and marks removed.

Compilation F1 Decision

Jeollok tonggo 0.940 kept
Sugyo jimnok 0.936 kept (the 7 extra splits are entries grouped with circles)

Overall F1 was 0.870, below the rule fixed beforehand (0.9). In the Jeonyul tongbo the coder's extra splits never exceed the number of circles inside notes in each jomok: the input mixed text and notes. This site uses level4 nodes that carry text (1,639 provisions) and also gives the count adding the circled items in their notes (4,471).
