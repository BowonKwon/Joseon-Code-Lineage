Full contents: chapters and sections

In short. After the Daejeon hoetong, Joseon made no new great code in the six-jeon form. In 1867 came the Yukjeon jorye (preface 1866), a collection of the practice of 100 offices of the capital; with 335,379 characters in its six jeon (notes included), it is the largest book counted on this site. In 1894 a decree on official documents (公文式) laid down how laws and decrees were to be issued, and in 1905 the Hyeongbeop daejeon (刑法大全), a penal code, was promulgated. In the Gojong and Sunjong Veritable Records, 大典會通 appears in 47 articles from 1865 to 1907, and 刑法大全 in 25 articles from 1905 to 1908.

The Yukjeon jorye: the practice of the offices (1867)#

According to the Gojong Veritable Records for the 17th day of the 12th lunar month of 1865, once the Daejeon hoetong had been issued, the compilation office proposed to go on gathering the practice of the government offices of the capital, on the model of the Collected Statutes (會典), under the name Yukjeon jorye (六典條例). The preface is dated 上之三年冬十一月, winter 1866. It says that although the Daejeon hoetong was complete, some detailed rules to be applied were still missing (應行條式, 尙或闕遺). The Veritable Records note that printing was finished and the book issued on the 16th day of the 5th lunar month of 1867.

The book has ten volumes: two each for Personnel, Revenue, Rites and Military, one each for Punishments and Works. Each volume is arranged by office — 宗親府, 司憲府, 戶曹 and so on — and records its posts and working practice. Where the Daejeon hoetong is a code divided by jomok (subjects), the Yukjeon jorye is closer to a handbook of the offices.

Counted in the open data of the National Institute of Korean History, it has 100 offices once the list at the head of each volume is left out. Below the offices there are 970 last-level items that carry text ("leaf items"). This site counts the offices as jomok and the leaf items as provisions. Because a jomok here is an office, it is not the same kind of unit as the jomok (subjects) of the other compilations. A leaf item holds several pieces divided by ○: there are 3,826 ○ in the main text and 2,377 in the notes.

Revenue is the largest part and Punishments, at 3.8%, the smallest: it is a single volume with three offices. The notes hold more characters than the main text (201,476 against 133,903). Volume heading lines (such as 六典條例卷之一) and the lists are not counted.

A new form for rules (1894–1895)#

During the Gabo reforms, on the 21st day of the 11th lunar month of 1894, the Gojong Veritable Records record that Decree No. 1 sanctioned and promulgated a decree on official documents (公文式) and abolished the earlier rules for issuing documents. On 8 May 1895 (lunar) Decree No. 86 promulgated the decree on official documents again; its first article provides that laws and decrees are issued by royal edict (上諭). The decree set the forms in which laws, decrees and other orders were issued — a different path from adding provisions to a great code and compiling it anew, which is the line this site follows. Laws and decrees after the Gabo reforms are not counted in this version.

The Hyeongbeop daejeon (1905)#

The Gojong Veritable Records for 29 April 1905 (solar calendar) record that the Hyeongbeop daejeon (刑法大全) was completed and promulgated throughout the country. The promulgation says the code was based on the laws of the former kings and drew on foreign rules. A month later, on 31 May, a committee for drafting laws was set up because, while the penal code had been issued, there were as yet no settled rules on civil law. From the article of 24 June onwards, judgments in the Veritable Records cite the Hyeongbeop daejeon by article number, as in "Article 631".

The Hyeongbeop daejeon is a penal code with numbered articles. In the open data of the National Institute of Korean History, 刑法大全 has 5 parts, 17 chapters and 162 sections with 680 articles (2 of them supplementary), and 41,616 characters in its articles. The edict, notes on use and table of contents at its head, and the Law No. 3 (法律第三號) bound at the end, are not counted. This arrangement by part, chapter, section and article differs from the jeon and jomok of the great codes. Compilation in the six-jeon form, laying the layers 原, 續, 增 and 補 on top of the earlier great codes, ended with the Daejeon hoetong.

In the Veritable Records#

大典會通 appears in 0 to 6 articles a year from its promulgation to 1907; the highest years are 1899 (6) and 1865 (5). 刑法大全 appears from 1905. Both names appear in the same year (not necessarily in the same article) only in 1905 and 1907.

Not done in this chapter#

  • Only the structure and size of the Hyeongbeop daejeon were counted; its content and any match with provisions of the earlier codes were not examined.
  • Laws and decrees after the Gabo reforms were not counted; the chapter stops where the line of the codes ends.
  • Under their terms of use (KOGL Type 4), the Gojong and Sunjong Veritable Records are used only for counts and dates, never quoted.
