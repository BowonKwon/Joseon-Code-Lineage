Full contents: chapters and sections

Key point. The instructions of the 150-odd years after the Daejeon husongnok were gathered in the Sugyo jimnok (受敎輯錄) of 1698, followed in the early eighteenth century by the Sinbo sugyo jimnok (新補受敎輯錄). Counting dated instruction strings by reign, the Sugyo jimnok has most under Sukjong (266) and Hyeonjong (165), the Sinbo sugyo jimnok under Sukjong (571) and Yeongjo (356). Each of three books has a single date from the years of Gwanghaegun.

The preface of the Sugyo jimnok was written by Yi Yeo (李畬) in the first third of the third month of 1698. It notes that "the Husongnok was completed in the gyemyo year of Jungjong, and since then another hundred and fifty years and more have passed" (「後續錄之成, 在於中廟癸卯, 其後至今, 又一百五十有餘年矣」). The editorial notes (凡例) say that instructions are entered by year and month, that "matters of the same kind are entered together, separated by circles" (「事之相類者, 則作圈合錄」), and that earlier and later instructions are all included even where they contradict each other (「一倂輯錄」).

The Jeollok tonggo (典錄通考) is a different kind of book. Its preface was written by Choe Seok-jeong (崔錫鼎) in 1706, and the last volume ends with "printed in the ninth month of the jeonghae year" (1707). Its editorial notes say that "the Great Code is like a classic, the three Rok like commentaries" (「大典如經書, 三錄如傳·註」), and so it sets the provisions of the Songnok, Husongnok and Sugyo jimnok under the provisions of the Gyeongguk daejeon. This book and its enlarged edition therefore reprint the code and earlier instructions. Their size cannot be compared on one line with the collections of instructions.

The open data holds no preface or postface for the Sinbo sugyo jimnok or the Jeungbo jeollok tonggo, and this site has not checked their years in the original. The latest date in both is 1738. The Sinbo sugyo jimnok has been dated by estimate to about 1739 (Yeongjo 15) (Encyclopedia of Korean Culture, entry 신보수교집록). The Veritable Records of Yeongjo carry a memorial by the Minister of Punishments on the 24th day of the 10th month of Yeongjo 8 (1732), proposing a sequel that gathers the instructions issued after the Sugyo jimnok, and a royal order on the 5th day of the 3rd month of Yeongjo 19 (1743) to continue the Sugyo jimnok and have it printed. The book was not printed at the time (Kim 2009, p. 223). This site takes none of these years as the year of compilation and places the book at its latest date, 1738, in the charts. ⚠ The provision count of the Jeungbo jeollok tonggo rests on an unstable unit (see Reliability).

Terms — the six jeon (六典): the six parts into which the codes and the collections of instructions divide their text: Personnel (吏典), Revenue (戶典), Rites (禮典), Military (兵典), Punishments, i.e. the Penal Code (刑典), and Works (工典). Jomok (條目): a headed section under a jeon. Provision: a single rule under a jomok (where one ends differs by book; see Method). Sugyo (受敎): a royal instruction.

Instructions by reign#

The dated strings are split by reign, using the first regnal year (元年) given in each reign's Sillok headings: a year belongs to the king whose first year is the latest one not after it.

From the years of Gwanghaegun (1609–1622) the Sugyo jimnok, Jeollok tonggo and Jeungbo jeollok tonggo each hold one dated string — very few next to Seonjo before (63) and Injo after (54). This site has not looked into why.

The reign shares of the Sinbo sugyo jimnok come in the same order as the figures quoted by Kim Baek-cheol (2009); the sizes differ. This site counts dated strings, Kim counts instructions.

Reading the dates#

Most dates are written as era name and sexagenary year, as in 「康熙壬戌承傳」. When a sexagenary year falls twice in one era (康熙壬寅 is both 1662 and 1722), the year cannot be fixed from the string alone. This site sets it in this order:

  1. Where the National Institute of Korean History corrected the text in brackets 〔 〕, the correction is followed (13).
  2. Where the same provision appears in another compilation, its date there is used (11).
  3. The year written by the Institute's editors in the node title is used (29).
  4. A year later than the checked year of compilation is dropped (16; the fifteen 康熙壬寅 of the Jeollok tonggo become 1662).

The remaining 31 (one of them dated differently in two compilations) are left out. The Sugyo jimnok (preface 1698) has one 康熙丙申 (1716) and one 康熙戊戌 (1718); the same strings appear in the Jeollok tonggo printed in 1707. A book of 1707 cannot hold an instruction of 1716, so these are taken as copying errors and left out.

In the Veritable Records#

「受敎輯錄」 appears in 3 articles under Sukjong, 6 under Yeongjo and 2 under Jeongjo; 「典錄通考」 in 5 under Yeongjo. 「新補受敎輯錄」 and 「增補典錄通考」 do not appear.

Jeong Geung-sik (2020) collected 1,079 instructions of the Ministry of Punishments (484 without duplicates) from fourteen late-Joseon collections and found 71.3% from Yeongjo and Jeongjo (figures from the abstract). The unit differs from the strings counted here.
