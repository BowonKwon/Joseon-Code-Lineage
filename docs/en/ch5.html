Full contents: chapters and sections

Key point. The Jeonyul tongbo (典律通補) of 1786 named a source for every provision. Divided by those marks, the 143,565 characters of its six jeon come 26.6% from the Sok daejeon, the largest share, 15.6% from the Ming Code, and 10.6% from the Gyeongguk daejeon alone; 19.2% carry two or more marks. In the Penal Code the Ming Code is the largest source, at 42.8%.

Compiled privately, revised at royal command#

The postface of the Jeonyul tongbo was written by Gu Yun-myeong (具允明) in the eighth month of 1786 (「歲丙午八月」). By his account he first put the book together "to keep in a box and consult on my own" (「藏之巾衍, 私自考閱」); in the ninth month of Jeongjo's ninth year (1785) the king summoned him to revise it. The editorial notes say the book rests on the Gyeongguk daejeon, the Sok daejeon, the Daejeon tongpyeon and the Ming Code, and that the provisions newly added in the Tongpyeon are "all entered" (「盡錄之」), adding at once that "a few of no importance are left out" (「惟無關緊者, 數條, 略之」).

Thirteen source marks#

"Every provision must carry a note of its source" (「諸條, 必懸註其出處」), say the editorial notes, and they define each mark.

Mark Source Mark Source
經 Gyeongguk daejeon 五禮 Five Rites (Orye ui)
續 Sok daejeon (with its supplement) 續禮 Sequel to the Five Rites
增 Daejeon tongpyeon 補編 Sangnye bopyeon
律 Ming Code (with commentaries and appended rules) 館志 Tongmungwan ji
欽恤 Heumhyul jeonchik 解 Daejeon juhae
補 royal instructions and registers 無冤 Muwollok
決訟 Gyeolsong yuchwi

On the page a mark is a small character in a rectangular frame, placed after the text it covers. Here 補 means "from royal instructions and registers" (「出於受敎及謄錄者」), not the 補 of the Daejeon hoetong (additions of 1865). This site keeps the two apart.

The sources named in the Jeonyul tongbo#

Terms — the six jeon (六典): the six parts into which the codes and the collections of instructions divide their text: Personnel (吏典), Revenue (戶典), Rites (禮典), Military (兵典), Punishments, i.e. the Penal Code (刑典), and Works (工典). Jomok (條目): a headed section under a jeon. Provision: a single rule under a jomok (where one ends differs by book; see Method). Sugyo (受敎): a royal instruction.

The figures depend on how unmarked text is read. Since the notes say every provision carries a source, this site reads unmarked text as taking the next mark in the same jomok (carry). On the Kyujanggak page images, all 16 places that are unmarked in the data are unmarked on the page as well. At the 14 places where the next mark was also checked on the page, it is the one the carry reading gives; for the other 2 the next page was not opened (see Reliability). Reading text left at the end of a node with that node's first mark instead (resume) moves the shares by 0.6 percentage points at most.

Text carrying two or more marks (19.2%) is counted on its own. The notes say mixed sources within one provision are marked together (「一條之內, 出處錯互者, 竝註之」) and that where a key word was changed (a 「緊字」, such as 監役 becoming 參軍) 續 and 增 are both given. Splitting such text evenly among its sources gives the Sok daejeon 32.4%; counting every segment that carries the 續 mark at all gives 40.4%. Where the same mark is printed twice over one segment (續+續+補, 177 characters), it counts as one source. The most frequent combination is 續+補 (158 segments, 5,420 characters).

These shares describe the sources the compilers named in 1786. They do not tell when a provision was first made: a provision marked 續 may go back to an instruction of the Gyeongguk daejeon period.

Each jeon differs#

In the Penal Code the Ming Code has 42.8% and the Sok daejeon 34.1%; the rule that the Ming Code applies (「用律, 依原典, 用大明律」) is stated under the offices of that code, with the proviso that where the Gyeongguk daejeon or the Sok daejeon has a matching rule, those two codes prevail (「而原典·續典有當律者, 從二典」). The Personnel Code has the largest share marked 經 (26.0%); the Revenue Code is dominated by the Sok daejeon (36.7%) and royal instructions and registers (22.2%). The Rites Code draws on ritual books: Sangnye bopyeon (11.1%), Tongmungwan ji (5.4%) and the Five Rites (4.5%).

The Separate Volume#

The Separate Volume (別編) gathers tables, forms and maps (37,754 characters). The notes say that only 經, 續, 增 and the Five Rites are marked there, other sources are not, and marks are not given paragraph by paragraph as in the main text. Where provisions from royal instructions are mixed in among them and hard to separate, those are marked as well. On the page, marks appear only after the titles of forms and in some notes. It is left out of the shares above.

In the Veritable Records#

「大典通編」 appears in 44 articles under Jeongjo and 9 under Sunjo. 「典律通補」 never appears.
