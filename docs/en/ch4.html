Chapter 4. The Sok daejeon (1744–1746)

In short. The Sok daejeon (續大典) was completed in 1744 and printed in 1746. It did not replace the Gyeongguk daejeon; it gathered what that code did not contain, so the code of 1746 is the two books together. Its six jeon hold 71,854 characters, close to the 68,775 of the Gyeongguk daejeon, but its Penal and Revenue codes are more than twice as large and its Personnel Code about half. Of the 1865 text under the mark 續, 76 to 91% is found in the Sok daejeon, depending on how the marks are read.

The first article of the editorial notes (凡例) says that what the Gyeongguk daejeon did not contain was all gathered here (「今就大典所不載者, 竝皆續輯」). Offices added or reduced are given by name with a note beneath; royal instructions are not cited under each provision, and dates are given only for special orders of great weight in punishment and government (「各條下, 勿書受敎 … 關係大段刑政特敎通變者, 則別書年月以識」).

The size of the two books

Terms — the six jeon (六典): the six parts into which the codes and the collections of instructions divide their text: Personnel (吏典), Revenue (戶典), Rites (禮典), Military (兵典), Punishments, i.e. the Penal Code (刑典), and Works (工典). Jomok (條目): a headed section under a jeon. Provision: a single rule under a jomok (where one ends differs by book; see Method). Sugyo (受敎): a royal instruction.

The six jeon of the Sok daejeon hold 71,854 characters and 1,713 provisions. Its Penal Code (16,387 characters) is more than twice that of the Gyeongguk daejeon (8,031), and its Revenue Code (14,790) 2.6 times (5,698). Its Personnel Code (8,610) is a little over half (15,997). The two books used together in 1746 hold 140,629 characters in their six jeon.

Twenty jomok titles occur in this book and not in the Gyeongguk daejeon (no jomok of the same title there); twelve of them are in the Penal Code. This points the same way as Chapter 6, where 11 of the 15 jomok of the Daejeon hoetong whose earliest mark is 續 are in the Penal Code.

Words for punishments in the Penal Code

Words for punishments in the Penal Code were counted with fixed character patterns (regular expressions, such as 杖 followed by a number) and divided per 10,000 characters. This counts how often a word appears in the text; it does not judge how severe the punishments were.

流 … 里 (exile) 6 (7.5) 31 (18.9) 42 (15.5)

Figures in brackets are per 10,000 characters of the Penal Code (8,031, 16,387 and 27,110 characters). 定配 never appears in the Penal Code of the Gyeongguk daejeon and appears 59 times in that of the Sok daejeon. For small counts (single figures in the Gyeongguk daejeon) the rate per 10,000 moves a great deal. 斬 excludes 斬衰, which names a mourning garment. The full list is on the Data page.

The Sok daejeon in the Daejeon hoetong

Set against the Daejeon hoetong, 1,294 of the 1,713 provisions of the Sok daejeon (75.5%) stand there with the same text (21 pairs of variant characters and punctuation aside). For 89 provisions less than half was found, 65 of them in the Personnel Code. Some provisions on posts there went into office tables in the later codes, where the layout changes. 'No match' mixes provisions really dropped with provisions the comparison did not find (the two were not measured apart), so this is not a count of provisions that disappeared.

The other way round, of the 1865 text under the mark 續, 76.4% is Sok daejeon text if a mark covers everything up to the next mark (carry reading), and 91.0% if a mark inside a small-print note covers only that note (nested reading). See the section 'Did the text follow the marks?' in Chapter 6.

Words that name a code

The text of the Sok daejeon uses 大典 119 times and 以 … 律論 ('judged by the article of … ') 121 times. 大典 refers to the Gyeongguk daejeon (大典 inside book titles such as 續大典 and 大典通編 is left out); 以 … 律論 calls on an article of the Ming Code.

Not done in this chapter

  • The provisions of the Sok daejeon were not paired with the provisions of the Gyeongguk daejeon they amend. Because the Sok daejeon gathered what the earlier code did not contain, the fate of the Gyeongguk daejeon provisions cannot be measured directly in 1746 (which is why the 1746 cell is left empty in Chapter 6).
  • The numbers of revised and new items given in encyclopedias and introductions were not compared: their unit differs from the provisions of this site.
